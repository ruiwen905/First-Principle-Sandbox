Our students memorise formulas, pass exams, and forget. The world they will enter requires them to derive the formula, disagree constructively, and navigate systems with no textbook answer. This proposal does not dismantle MOE's rigorous syllabus — it adds the missing half.

Pedagogical Foundation

Why First Principles Teaching Changes Everything

Traditional Method
Teach the formula A = πr²
Memorise for the exam
Score A, forget in 3 weeks
Teach the screwdriver, then find a bolt
One right answer per question
Reasoning by analogy: "this is how it's done"
Teacher = content delivery engine
First Principles / Astra Nova Way
Break a circle into infinite triangles — derive the formula yourself
Own the concept permanently because you understand why
Apply to any future shape, even one not yet invented
Hand students an engine. They ask for the screwdriver when needed
Multiple defensible positions — quality of reasoning graded
Ask "what is physically possible here?" regardless of convention
Teacher = facilitator of discovery and productive disagreement

Conundrum Library

8 per level · centralised by MOE
LP·01 · FAIRNESS
The Sick Friend Conundrum
Fairness & Need
Your friend is sick and missed the school trip. You have one souvenir. Do you keep it or give it to them? What if you saved up for it for a month?
LP·02 · ENVIRONMENT
The River Factory
Jobs vs. Nature
A factory near school makes great toys — but its dirty water is killing the fish. Your parents and friends' parents work there. Should it be closed?
LP·03 · SHARING
The Cookie Problem
Equal vs. Equitable
3 cookies, 4 friends. Do you cut them equally — or give more to the friend who didn't eat breakfast? Is fair always the same as equal?
LP·04 · HEALTH
The Doctor's Queue
Visible vs. Hidden Need
The school nurse has 5 kids waiting. One is crying loudly. One is very quiet but looks pale. Who goes first — the loudest or the sickest?
LP·05 · HONESTY
The Broken Toy
Blame vs. Collective Fix
The class shared toy breaks. Nobody admits it. Should everyone chip in to fix it — or wait for the person who broke it to own up?
LP·06 · EFFORT
The Helper's Reward
Individual Effort vs. Group Rules
One child cleaned the whole classroom alone while others played. Should she get extra recess time — even though the rule says recess is equal for all?
LP·07 · COMMUNITY
The Sunlight Garden
Priority & Ownership
There is one sunny spot in the school garden. Three children each planted a seed. One planted first, one grows fastest, one waters it every day. Whose plant deserves the sun?
LP·08 · KINDNESS
The Birthday Moment
Generosity vs. Personal Joy
One classmate has never had a birthday party. It's someone else's birthday today. Should she share her cake-cutting moment — even if today is her special day?
UP·01 · HEALTHCARE
The Free Clinic Slots
Access vs. Urgency
A free clinic has 10 slots. Families queue from 5am. Should slots go to who arrived first, who is sickest, or who lives farthest away?
UP·02 · ECONOMY
The Empty Provision Shop
Loyalty vs. Savings
A big supermarket opens nearby. It's cheaper. The old uncle's shop will close. Should the neighbourhood keep buying from the uncle even if it costs more?
UP·03 · INCOME GAP
The School Bursary
Comparing Hardship
One scholarship. Two needy students — one has a sick parent, one has four siblings. No money for both. How do you decide?
UP·04 · ENVIRONMENT
The Lake Conundrum ✦
Jobs vs. Ecosystem
A factory is the town's only employer but poisons the lake. Scientists say the lake dies in 10 years. Close it now and cause unemployment — or wait?
UP·05 · HEALTHCARE COST
The Faster Lane Hospital
Tiered Access vs. Equal Care
Rich patients can pay extra to see the doctor sooner — and the revenue funds more beds for the poor. Is a two-tier healthcare system fair if it helps everyone overall?
UP·06 · EDUCATION GAP
The Tuition Ban
Parental Rights vs. Systemic Equality
Kids with tutors always score higher. Should tuition be banned so everyone starts even — even if parents want to help their own children?
UP·07 · MEDICINE
The Free Formula
IP vs. Public Good
A scientist can share the formula for a $100 medicine so anyone can make it cheaply. The company that invented it says that's theft. Who is right?
UP·08 · NUTRITION
The Free School Meal
Universal Dignity vs. Targeted Efficiency
Universal free school meals cost $500M/yr and remove stigma. Or: give the money only to the poorest families. Which is the better use of public funds?
LS·01 · HEALTHCARE
The Surgery Lottery
Fairness of Process vs. Outcome
A country can fund 1,000 heart surgeries but 3,000 people need them. First-come, lottery, or prioritise younger patients who have more years ahead?
LS·02 · AUTOMATION
The Robot Nurse
Cost Reduction vs. Employment
AI diagnoses illness better than human doctors at 1/10th the cost. If hospitals switch to AI, care becomes affordable — but 50,000 nurses lose their jobs. Should they switch?
LS·03 · INCOME GAP
The CEO Pay Cap
Wage Equity vs. Talent Retention
A CEO earns 400× more than the lowest-paid worker. A law proposes capping CEO pay unless wages double. The CEO threatens to leave. Should the law pass?
LS·04 · CLIMATE
The Sugar Tax
Public Health vs. Regressive Cost
A sugar tax reduces obesity. But poor families spend a larger share of income on sugary drinks than the rich. Is the tax fair even if it helps national health?
LS·05 · ETHICS
The Organ Market
Supply of Life vs. Exploitation
Legal organ selling would save thousands of kidney patients yearly. But only poor people would sell. Should organ markets be legalised?
LS·06 · WEALTH
The Inheritance Wall
Family Rights vs. Structural Inequality
A child born rich starts with a $1M trust fund. A child born poor starts with debt. Should there be a cap on inherited wealth? Where is the line?
LS·07 · DATA
The Data Doctor
Privacy vs. Funding Equity
Your health data is worth $10,000/year to pharma companies. Should hospitals sell it to fund free care for the uninsured — without your explicit consent?
LS·08 · HOUSING
The Zoning Conundrum
Property Rights vs. Spatial Equity
Affordable housing is proposed in an expensive area. Residents protest — values will fall. The alternative: poor families commute 2 hours to work and school.
US·01 · AGI
The AGI Displacement Conundrum
Human Relevance in an AI World
AGI performs 80% of knowledge work by 2040. Do governments guarantee income and retrain citizens, or accept mass structural unemployment as the cost of progress?
US·02 · HEALTHCARE
The Longevity Gap
Life Extension Ethics
The wealthy already live 15 years longer. A new government-funded longevity drug extends life by 20 years. Should it be rationed to prevent the gap growing to 35 years?
US·03 · INCOME GAP
The Universal Basic Income Test
Guaranteed Floor vs. Work Incentives
A city gives every adult $1,000/month, no conditions. Some thrive. Some stop working. Taxes rise. Is UBI the solution to income inequality — or a trap?
US·04 · SINGAPORE
The Sovereign Wealth Paradox
Fiscal Conservatism vs. Present Welfare
Singapore's reserves could fund free universal healthcare for 50 years. Drawing it down weakens crisis resilience. At what point does saving for the future become neglect of the present?
US·05 · MERITOCRACY
The Meritocracy Myth
Valid Measurement vs. Systemic Bias
Standardised exams favour children of educated parents. But removing them means employers can't distinguish candidates fairly. Design a selection system that is both valid and equitable.
US·06 · PHARMA
The Pharma Dilemma
Rare Disease Equity vs. Scale of Impact
A company can cure a rare disease (10,000 patients) at $500K/dose — or treat a common disease (10M patients) at $50/dose. They can only fund one. How should governments incentivise the right choice?
US·07 · LABOUR
The Gig Economy Trap
Worker Protection vs. Market Flexibility
Gig workers have no CPF, sick leave, or job security. Classifying them as employees fixes this — but raises costs. Companies may exit, eliminating the jobs entirely.
US·08 · TRANSPARENCY
The Transparent Salary Law
Equity vs. Privacy & Retention
A law forces companies to publish all salaries publicly. Evidence shows it closes gender pay gaps. But it causes resentment, poaching, and some top performers quit. Should it be mandatory?
How Students Use AI
🔍
Research Phase (Day Before)
Students prompt AI assistants to gather multi-sided perspectives on the conundrum. Guided prompt templates provided by MOE: "Give me 3 arguments for and 3 against closing the factory. Cite real-world precedents."
⚖️
Steel-Manning Exercise
AI is used to argue the opposite of the student's position, forcing them to strengthen their own reasoning. The student must identify flaws in the AI's counterargument.
📊
Data & Context Loading
Students query AI for real statistics: "What % of Singapore's healthcare budget goes to chronic illness?" Facts grounded in real data raise the quality of debate.
📝
Post-Session Reflection
Students dictate or type a 100-word reflection. AI provides structured feedback on logical consistency — not on the position taken, but on the quality of argument. Logged to portfolio.
The Facilitator's Role
🎯
Not a Lecturer — A Climate Setter
The facilitator's job is to create psychological safety for disagreement. They do not deliver the "right answer." They hold the space where intellectual bravery is rewarded.
❓
Asks Deep First-Principles Questions
"What is the most fundamental reason you believe that?" / "What assumption are you making that might not be true?" / "What would change your mind?" MOE Playbook provides 50 such prompts per age tier.
🔄
Manages Group Dynamics
Assigns rotating roles: Proposer, Devil's Advocate, Evidence Checker, Synthesiser. Ensures no voice dominates. Observes and scores reasoning quality in real-time using a standardised rubric.
📋
Logs Micro-Credentials
After each session, the facilitator submits a 3-minute competency snapshot per student to the MOE National Competency Matrix. No essay marking — behavioural observation only.

Micro-Credentials
Continuous portfolio · Demo Days · Facilitator rubric
Information Synthesis
How effectively a student filters noise from a chaotic data set to identify core truths. Assessed via pre-session AI research quality.
Logic & Derivation
Ability to break a complex problem down to its physical laws or foundational truths. Scored during Conundrum debate.
Adaptive Pivot
How effectively a student changes strategy when new evidence challenges their position. Measured live by facilitator.
Constructive Disagreement
Capacity to challenge peers' reasoning without attacking their identity. Core social skill for every future workplace.
Ethical Reasoning Depth
Recognition of competing stakeholder interests and second-order consequences. Demonstrated in position papers and Demo Days.
