The Problem We Are Solving

Singapore produces high scorers.
But who builds the next thing?

#1PISA global ranking in Maths & Science
AGIProjected within 10–20 years. Rote skills become obsolete.
0Current formal curriculum hours on ethical reasoning

Our students memorise formulas, pass exams, and forget. The world they will enter requires them to derive the formula, disagree constructively, and navigate systems with no textbook answer. This proposal does not dismantle MOE's rigorous syllabus — it adds the missing half.

Pedagogical Foundation

Why First Principles Teaching Changes Everything

Traditional Method
Teach the formula A = πr²
Memorise for the exam
Score A, forget in 3 weeks
Teach the screwdriver, then find a bolt
One right answer per question
Reasoning by analogy: "this is how it's done"
Teacher = content delivery engine
First Principles / Astra Nova Way
Break a circle into infinite triangles — derive the formula yourself
Own the concept permanently because you understand why
Apply to any future shape, even one not yet invented
Hand students an engine. They ask for the screwdriver when needed
Multiple defensible positions — quality of reasoning graded
Ask "what is physically possible?" regardless of convention
Teacher = facilitator of discovery and productive disagreement

Conundrum Library

8 per level · centralised by MOE
LP·01
The Sick Friend Conundrum
Fairness & Need
Your friend missed the school trip because they were sick. You brought back one souvenir. Do you keep it or give it away? What if you saved up for it yourself?
LP·02
The River Factory
Jobs vs. Nature
A factory near your school makes great toys — but its dirty water is killing the fish in the river. Your friends' parents work there. Should the factory be closed?
LP·03
The Cookie Problem
Equal vs. Equitable
3 cookies, 4 friends. Do you cut them equally — or give more to the friend who skipped breakfast? Is fair always the same as equal?
LP·04
The Doctor's Queue
Visible vs. Hidden Need
The school nurse has 5 kids waiting. One is crying loudly. One is very quiet but looks pale. Who should go first — the loudest or the sickest?
LP·05
The Broken Toy
Blame vs. Collective Fix
The class shared toy breaks. Nobody admits it. Should everyone chip in to fix it — or wait for the person who broke it to own up?
LP·06
The Helper's Reward
Effort vs. Group Rules
One child cleaned the whole classroom alone while others played. Should she get extra recess — even though the rule says recess is equal for all?
LP·07
The Sunlight Garden
Priority & Ownership
One sunny spot in the school garden. Three children planted seeds. One planted first, one grows fastest, one waters it every day. Whose plant deserves the sun?
LP·08
The Birthday Moment
Generosity vs. Personal Joy
One classmate has never had a birthday party. It's someone else's birthday today. Should she share her cake-cutting moment — even though today is her special day?
UP·01
The Free Clinic Slots
Access vs. Urgency
A free clinic has 10 slots. Families queue from 5am. Should slots go to whoever arrived first, whoever is sickest, or whoever lives farthest away?
UP·02
The Empty Provision Shop
Loyalty vs. Savings
A big supermarket opens nearby. It's cheaper. The old uncle's shop will close. Should the neighbourhood keep buying from him even if it costs more?
UP·03
The School Bursary
Comparing Hardship
One scholarship. Two needy students — one has a sick parent, one has four siblings. No budget for both. How do you decide who gets it?
UP·04
The Lake Conundrum ✦
Jobs vs. Ecosystem
A factory is the town's only employer but poisons the lake. Scientists say it dies in 10 years. Close it now and cause unemployment — or wait?
UP·05
The Faster Lane Hospital
Tiered Access vs. Equal Care
Rich patients pay extra to skip the queue — the revenue funds more beds for the poor. Is a two-tier healthcare system fair if it helps everyone overall?
UP·06
The Tuition Ban
Parental Rights vs. Equity
Kids with tutors always score higher. Should tuition be banned so everyone starts even — even though parents want to help their own children?
UP·07
The Free Formula
IP vs. Public Good
A scientist can share the formula for a $100 medicine so anyone can make it cheaply. The company that invented it says that's theft. Who is right?
UP·08
The Free School Meal
Universal Dignity vs. Efficiency
Universal free school meals cost $500M/yr and remove stigma. Or: give the money only to the poorest families. Which is the better use of public funds?
LS·01
The Surgery Lottery
Fairness of Process vs. Outcome
A country can fund 1,000 heart surgeries but 3,000 need them. First-come-first-served, a lottery, or prioritise younger patients with more years ahead?
LS·02
The Robot Nurse
Cost Reduction vs. Employment
AI diagnoses illness better than doctors at 1/10th the cost. If hospitals switch, care becomes affordable — but 50,000 nurses lose their jobs. Should they switch?
LS·03
The CEO Pay Cap
Wage Equity vs. Talent Retention
A CEO earns 400× more than the lowest-paid worker. A law caps CEO pay unless wages double first. The CEO threatens to leave. Should the law pass?
LS·04
The Sugar Tax
Public Health vs. Regressive Cost
A sugar tax reduces obesity. But poor families spend a larger share of income on sugary drinks than the rich. Is the tax fair even if it improves national health?
LS·05
The Organ Market
Supply of Life vs. Exploitation
Legal organ selling would save thousands of kidney patients yearly. But only poor people would sell. Should organ markets be legalised?
LS·06
The Inheritance Wall
Family Rights vs. Structural Inequality
A child born rich starts life with a $1M trust fund. A child born poor starts with debt. Should there be a legal cap on inherited wealth?
LS·07
The Data Doctor
Privacy vs. Funding Equity
Your health data is worth $10,000/yr to pharma companies. Should hospitals sell it to fund free care for the uninsured — without your explicit consent?
LS·08
The Zoning Conundrum
Property Rights vs. Spatial Equity
Affordable housing is proposed in an expensive area. Residents protest — values will fall. The alternative: poor families commute 2 hours to work and school daily.
US·01
The AGI Displacement Conundrum
Human Relevance in an AI World
AGI performs 80% of knowledge work by 2040. Do governments guarantee income and retrain citizens, or accept mass structural unemployment as the price of progress?
US·02
The Longevity Gap
Life Extension Ethics
The wealthy already live 15 years longer. A government-funded longevity drug adds 20 more years. Should it be rationed to prevent the gap growing to 35 years?
US·03
The UBI Test
Guaranteed Floor vs. Work Incentives
A city gives every adult $1,000/month, no conditions. Some thrive. Some stop working. Taxes rise for all. Is UBI the solution to income inequality — or a trap?
US·04
The Sovereign Wealth Paradox
Fiscal Conservatism vs. Present Welfare
Singapore's reserves could fund free universal healthcare for 50 years. Drawing it down weakens crisis resilience. At what point does saving for the future become neglect of the present?
US·05
The Meritocracy Myth
Valid Measurement vs. Systemic Bias
Standardised exams favour children of educated parents. But removing them means employers can't distinguish candidates. Design a selection system that is both valid and equitable.
US·06
The Pharma Dilemma
Rare Disease Equity vs. Scale of Impact
A company can cure a rare disease (10K patients) at $500K/dose — or treat a common disease (10M patients) at $50/dose. They fund only one. How should governments incentivise the right choice?
US·07
The Gig Economy Trap
Worker Protection vs. Market Flexibility
Gig workers have no CPF, sick leave, or security. Classifying them as employees fixes this but raises costs — companies may exit, eliminating the jobs entirely. What do you do?
US·08
The Transparent Salary Law
Equity vs. Privacy & Retention
A law forces companies to publish all salaries publicly. Evidence shows it closes gender pay gaps. But it causes resentment and some top performers quit. Should it be mandatory?
Learning Tools & Roles
How Students Use AI
🔍
Research Phase (Day Before)
Students prompt AI to gather multi-sided perspectives. MOE prompt templates: "Give me 3 arguments for and 3 against. Cite real-world precedents."
⚖️
Steel-Manning Exercise
AI argues the opposite of the student's position, forcing them to strengthen their own reasoning and find flaws in counterarguments.
📊
Data & Context Loading
Students query AI for real statistics: "What % of Singapore's healthcare budget goes to chronic illness?" Real data raises debate quality.
📝
Post-Session Reflection
Students write a 100-word reflection. AI gives structured feedback on logical consistency — not on position taken, but on quality of argument. Logged to portfolio.
The Facilitator's Role
🎯
Not a Lecturer — A Climate Setter
The facilitator's job is to create psychological safety for disagreement. They do not deliver the "right answer." Intellectual bravery is rewarded.
❓
Asks First-Principles Questions
"What is the most fundamental reason you believe that?" / "What assumption might not be true?" MOE Playbook provides 50 such prompts per age tier.
🔄
Manages Group Dynamics
Assigns rotating roles: Proposer, Devil's Advocate, Evidence Checker, Synthesiser. Ensures no voice dominates. Observes and scores reasoning in real time.
📋
Logs Micro-Credentials
After each session, a 3-minute competency snapshot per student is submitted to the MOE National Competency Matrix. Behavioural observation — no essay marking.
Assessment Architecture

50/50. Not a compromise — a statement of values.

PSLE / O-Level weightings adjusted proportionally
50%
National Competency Matrix Micro-Credentials
Continuous portfolio · Demo Days · Facilitator rubric
Information Synthesis
How effectively a student filters noise from a chaotic data set to identify core truths. Assessed via pre-session AI research quality.
Logic & Derivation
Ability to break a complex problem down to its foundational truths. Scored during Conundrum debate.
Adaptive Pivot
How effectively a student changes strategy when new evidence challenges their position. Measured live by facilitator.
Constructive Disagreement
Capacity to challenge peers' reasoning without attacking their identity. Core skill for every future workplace.
Ethical Reasoning Depth
Recognition of competing stakeholder interests and second-order consequences. Demonstrated in position papers and Demo Days.

Singapore's PISA rank is extraordinary. But the skills that earned it — memorisation, pattern recognition, procedural fluency — are precisely what AGI will make abundant and cheap. The next competitive advantage is the one thing machines cannot fake: genuine ethical reasoning, adaptive thinking, and the courage to disagree well.
